**[RECORD THE SESSION — confirm consent]**

> We're mapping the [end-to-end scope, e.g. from product planning to raw materials to supplier to logistics to customer fulfilment] for [business]. I understand your team plays a key role in [area], so [referrer role] suggested I speak with you to understand how it works from your perspective and where it fits in the broader chain. We're mapping how the work actually happens, not checking on anyone; nothing will be linked to your name.

## Interviewer principles

1. Story before explanation. Ask what happened, not how it works. "How does it work?" gets the procedure; "What happened last time?" gets the practice.
2. Gather without judging. No analytical or evaluative questions early; collect what people actually decide and do, in their words.
3. Oblique before direct. For sensitive ground, ask for the story a new colleague should hear, not "what goes wrong".
4. Their words first. Note the SME's terms and reuse them in later questions ("the inefficient disconnect, as you call it"); never correct them.
5. "What happened next?" before "why?" Save "why" for the contrast movement (M6).
6. Silent listening. Don't fill pauses; the useful detail often follows one.
7. Follow a good story over the script. The close (M7) catches what's missed.
8. Documents last. Never mention the SOP's version before M6.
9. Present contrasts without evaluation. When playing back differences (M6, follow-ups, validation), describe them and let the SME draw the conclusion.
10. Session targets: at least three stories (went well, went badly, surprise); every decision deepened once; every handoff named; the timeline read back once; strengths as well as weaknesses.

## M4 — Decisions: decision mapping and ASHEN (12 min)

Start with the M4.0 sweep, then deepen 4–6 decisions in process order with M4.1–M4.8; the rest go to a follow-up.

| ID | Question | Probes | Minutes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M4.0 | *Decision sweep:* What decisions do you make daily? Weekly? Monthly? Once a year? Only when something unusual happens? | However small | 1½ |  |
| M4.1 | *For each chosen decision:* At that point, what were your options? | Any you ruled out? | 1½ |  |
| M4.2 | What information did you receive, and which of it did you actually pay attention to? | What would have made you go the other way? | 1½ |  |
| M4.3 | Is there a rule for this, or does it depend? On what? | How often does the rule not fit? | 1½ |  |
| M4.4 | Who has the final say, whose advice do you seek, and who do you tell? | Who would you call if unsure? | 1½ |  |
| M4.5 | *ASHEN:* What did you use — documents, screens, tools? (A) · What would someone need to learn to do this well? (S) · Do you have a rule of thumb? (H) · Was there a time that taught you how to handle this? (E) · Does this role need a particular knack? (N, role level only) | Ask each once, in the SME's words | 1½ |  |
| M4.6 | How is the decision communicated or put into action, and who picks it up? | In what form — email, system, conversation? | 1 |  |
| M4.7 | What information, if you had it, would make this decision better? | Who has it today? | 1 |  |
| M4.8 | What would a new joiner probably get wrong here? What if the usual person were away? | What would you tell them? | 1 |  |
