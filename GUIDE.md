# Process Map Studio — Owner's Guide
*Updated: 2026-10-06 (version 1.0.0, with the standard interview templates and tailored interview guides)*

This guide explains how to **use** the app. How to install, start, stop, update, move and back it up is in [README.md](README.md).

## What this app does
You give it the documents behind one process: an SME interview transcript, SOPs, job descriptions and supporting documents. Claude reads them and drafts a swimlane map of how the work is really done: steps, roles, who is responsible and accountable, decisions, approvals, timings, KPIs and constraints. Every step links to the exact passage it came from. The app checks the AI's work before you see it, and anything it can't find in your documents is thrown out and listed for you. You review every step, and nothing is published until you have.

## What you can do today
With Process Map Studio you can start it, save your name and your Claude API key, create an engagement for each process, describe its scope and roles, upload documents and interview transcripts and read them inside the app, map each interview speaker to a role, keep a list of names the app hides, and **create a draft map**: Claude reads every source, every claim it makes is checked against your documents, and the checked claims are grouped into steps, compared source by source, checked a second time by the independent verifier and built into a draft map with questions for your SME. On **Review** you see the draft as a swimlane map or a table, open any step's details, follow each piece of evidence to the exact passage, and **review every step**: confirm it, edit it, reject it or add a step that is missing. Questions for your SME, rejected claims, the audit log, merge decisions and the map checks sit in tabs under the map. **Refresh draft from sources** redrafts only the steps you haven't reviewed. When every step is reviewed and the **quality card** is green, **Publish** the map: it becomes read-only on **Published**, and **Exports** gives it to you as the Master Process Map, a JSON file, a RACI spreadsheet and a single-file interactive map. Steps can also carry **pictures** (photos, screenshots, completed forms): you add them yourself, and pictures inside your PDFs are suggested on the steps whose evidence comes from the same page. Pictures stay inside the app and never go into an export. **Sources** shows how much of each file the map uses; **Matrix** compares every step with every kind of source; **Registers** gathers the knowledge risks, AI flags, constraints, recommended practices, questions, rejected claims and the **cause library** in one place. When you want a second opinion, the optional **Gap analysis** compares what your SMEs describe with the SOPs and job descriptions, and (with web research switched on and a research brief you approve) compares your map with published practice in your industry. Every finding comes with its evidence or sources, and nothing changes on the map unless you decide so. **Leadership** sums up a published map for senior readers in three columns kept apart: the facts (time, waiting, rework and handoffs per phase), the interpretation (which causes are systemic and how well your sources support them) and the recommendation (what could be won back, the AI flags and the decisions leadership needs to make). Every figure opens the steps behind it.

**Start and stop** (full steps in [README.md](README.md)): double-click **Start.command** (Mac) or **start.bat** (Windows). Your browser opens the app at http://127.0.0.1:8000. Close the small window to stop it.

### The home screen (Engagements)
- Until you have saved a key and created an engagement, a **Get started** card lists three steps and ticks each one off: add your Claude API key, create an engagement, upload sources.
- Each engagement has a card: its name, its latest map version (or "No draft yet"), how many steps are reviewed, how many sources it has and when it last changed. Click anywhere on the card to open the engagement; the app takes you to the step that needs you next.
- **New engagement** asks for a name (usually the process, for example "Aircraft load control") and opens its Setup screen.
- **Delete** on a card asks first and says exactly what will be removed: the engagement, its source files, roles, drafts and published versions. This can't be undone.
- **Interview templates** (a card on the home screen, always there, even before you create an engagement): the three standard interview templates, each with **Download Word** and **Download Markdown**. See "Interview templates, step by step" below.

### Interview templates, step by step
Every interviewer uses the same questions, the same question IDs and the same minute plan, so interviews are consistent and the app can link each answer to its question.
1. Choose the template for the person you are interviewing:
   - **Landscape (60 min)** for team leads, contract holders and cross-functional owners: which teams are involved, what each manages, the handoffs between them, who decides, strengths, weaknesses and risks.
   - **Practice (60 min)** for the people who do the work: the last real case step by step, the forms they use, the decisions they make, stories, and how the work compares with the procedure (only at the end).
   - **Follow-up (30 min)** for anyone you have already interviewed: blank rows for "You mentioned previously…" questions in their own words, then the close.
2. Download it from the home screen card or from the engagement's **Interview** screen, as **Word** (to print or type into) or **Markdown**. Each template shows the question bank version at the top.
3. Fill in the header table (engagement, the interviewee's **role**, stakeholder group, date, interviewer, who referred you) and tick **Recording consent** once they agree.
4. Start with **[RECORD THE SESSION]** and read the opener; replace the [bracketed] parts with your scope and roles.
5. Follow the minute plan. Each table lists the question **ID**, the question, follow-up **probes**, the suggested **minutes** and a **Notes** column for what you hear. In the story sections, ask the short questions under each story ("in story" means their time is inside the story's minutes).
6. **Say or note the question ID when you ask it**, for example "[M2.2] Walk me through what happened, step by step." With the ID in the transcript, the app links each answer to the question that produced it; if you forget, it matches the question by its wording instead, as long as you read it close to the template.
7. Use roles, not names ("the ramp supervisor", not a person's name). The documents and the SOP come in only at **M6** (Practice), so the SME describes the work as it really happens first.
8. Tick the **coverage checklist** at the end and note the next steps: the three completed examples to send, who else to talk to and the follow-up date.
9. Upload the recording's transcript on **Sources**; the **Interview** step in the top bar then shows as done.

### Tailored interview guides, step by step
A tailored guide is the standard template for one interviewee, with the parts you would otherwise fill in by hand already filled in. The question IDs, the questions and the minute plan are exactly those of the template, so you use the IDs in the same way.
1. Open the engagement → **Interview** → **Guide** tab. Under **Interviewees**, click **Add interviewee** and choose the interview type, the role or the stakeholder group (never a person's name), who referred you (by role) and, for a follow-up, the earlier interview.
2. Click **Make guide** next to the interviewee. It takes a few seconds (you see what it is doing); when it says **Guide version 1**, click **Download Word** (or **Download Markdown**). If the map or the interviewee changes, click **Make new version**; the downloads always give the latest version.
3. What is filled in for you: the title and header table (process, role, stakeholder group, referred by; you still add the date, your name and the consent tick), and the opener's [bracketed] parts (scope from Setup, the business, the area, who referred you).
4. **Landscape:** Claude drafts the **role module**: three to five questions (IDs **RM1**–**RM5**) about the interviewee's group's own handoffs, decisions and boundaries with the other teams. The app keeps only questions that are plain and open, end with a question mark, have one to three probes, use no blaming words, say nothing about what the documents say and contain no hidden name; the message under the interviewee says how many it kept. If Claude can't be reached (or no key is saved) the guide is still made, with the role-module rows left blank and the reason shown.
5. **Practice:** under **M6.5** (only there, after the person has described the work in their own words) the guide adds up to five questions about steps that only the documents describe, for example "The procedure mentions "Approve load plan". Where does that happen in practice?"
6. **Follow-up:** the blank rows take one question in turn from each of: the person's own words from the map ("You mentioned previously that…", question **F2**), what the coverage report shows as thin or missing (the question bank's question, with the step it is about), open questions from the review (**F6**) and gaps the map marks "not described" (**F5**). When you chose an earlier interview, the opener says what earlier interviews covered.
7. Anything that contains a name on your hidden-names list, or a blaming word, is never filled in: the [bracket] or the blank row stays for you.
8. **Edit guide** opens the guide as one card per section: change any question's wording or probes (one per line), or write your own questions into the blank rows (they get IDs **E1**, **E2**, …). The procedure questions in M6.5 are shown apart, "held back to avoid anchoring the SME". **Save guide** checks your changes with the same rules (no blaming words, no hidden names, nothing about what the documents say before M6) and shows any problem under the question; the downloads then give your edited version. Question IDs and minutes can't change, so the plan still adds up. **Make new version** starts again from the template and keeps your edited version.
9. During the interview, say or note each question ID as you ask it, exactly as with the templates. Two example guides are in `docs/samples/` (`interview-guide-practice-sample.docx` and `interview-guide-follow-up-sample.docx`).

### Inside an engagement: the guided path
When an engagement is open, the top bar shows its name and eleven steps: Setup · Interview · Sources · Draft · Review · Published · Matrix · Gap analysis · Registers · Leadership · Exports. Each has a small mark and a spoken label:
- **filled circle:** done;
- **ring with a number:** needs attention (the number says how many things to resolve);
- **empty circle:** not started.

The step to do next carries a **Next** tag. Gap analysis is marked **Optional** and is never suggested as next.

**Interview** counts as done once an interview transcript has been uploaded; until then it is the next step after Setup, and its screen has three tabs: **Templates** (the three standard templates), **Coverage** (see below) and **Guide**, where **Interviewees** lists whose interviews you are preparing: click **Add interviewee**, choose the interview type (Landscape, Practice or Follow-up), the role or the stakeholder group (never a person's name; the app refuses names on your hidden-names list), who referred you (by role) and, for a follow-up, the earlier interview it builds on. Each interviewee then has **Make guide** and, once made, **Edit guide**, **Download Word** and **Download Markdown** (see "Tailored interview guides, step by step"). After a draft, **Coverage** shows, for each of 15 things a good interview covers (trigger, end point, steps, handoffs, decisions, approvals, exceptions, RACI, time, KPIs, artefacts, know-how, stories, official vs lived, SME terms), whether the interviews covered it (filled circle), thinly (half circle) or not at all (dashed ring), with the evidence count; below it, the ready-to-ask follow-up questions with **Copy** and **Download .md**, and **Create Follow-up guide**: choose whom to follow up with and the app adds the Follow-up interviewee and makes the guide (you then see it on the Guide tab).

### Setup, step by step
1. **Process scope** (left card): check the **Engagement name**, then fill in the **Trigger** (what starts the process) and **End point** (what marks it as finished). Setup counts as done once both are filled.
2. Add the other fields you know: process name, **In-scope roles**, **Out of scope**, **Process KPI**, **Industry** and **Adjacent industries**. For lists, type an entry and press Enter (or a comma); click the small × on a chip to remove it.
3. **Confidential terms:** add company, client, site and system names (for example your company's and customers' names). They are never sent to web research in the optional external analysis. They stay visible inside the app.
4. Click **Save scope**. If something is wrong, the message appears under the field concerned.
5. **Roles and lanes** (right card): add each role you already know under **Add a role**, with any other names the documents use for it (**Aliases**, for example "LC"). Tick **System role** for a system or tool rather than a person. Roles found in your sources are also added when the draft runs.
6. Put the lanes in order: drag a role onto another, or use **Move up** and **Move down**. The **Lane preview** shows the order the lanes will have on the map, top to bottom. **Edit** changes a role's name and aliases.
7. **Mapping conventions** (optional; the defaults suit most maps): **Granularity** (coarse, about 8–12 steps; standard, 12–20; fine, 20–25), **Step labels** (standard wording, verb and object; or SME wording, the interviewee's own everyday name for a step) and **Phase names** (SME terms or standard terms). Click **Save conventions**; they apply from the next draft or **Refresh draft from sources**, and Claude follows them unless the sources leave no other way, saying why.

### Sources, step by step
1. Open **Sources** in the top bar. Drop files on the large box, or click **Choose files**. The app reads Word (.docx), PDF (.pdf), text (.txt), subtitle (.vtt, .srt), spreadsheet (.xlsx, .csv) and saved email (.eml) files up to 50 MB each; anything else is refused with that list. Spreadsheets and emails are **artefacts**: records of the work as it was actually done, like a filled-in form. The app counts them with executed artefacts when it compares sources.
2. Each file appears in the table straight away with the status **Reading…**, and changes to **Ready** a moment later (or **Failed** with the reason). You don't need to wait on the screen: if you close the app while a file is being read, it finishes when you start it again. The app guesses each file's **Kind** from its name (for example "jd" → Job description, "interview" → Interview transcript, a spreadsheet → Working spreadsheet, an .eml file → Email thread); change it if the guess is wrong. Changing a file to or from **Interview transcript** reads it again the right way.
3. Fill in the **Version date**, **Owner** and **Controlled copy** where you know them. Changes save as soon as you leave the field.
4. Click a file's name (or its row) to open the **source viewer** below the table. It lists every passage with its anchor: **§3.1** (a clause), **p. 5** (a PDF page), **¶ 7** (a paragraph), **06:40** (the time in an interview), **Plan!A5** (a spreadsheet's sheet and row, as Excel writes it) or **row 5** (a CSV file's row). A spreadsheet row reads "Header: value | Header: value", so each figure keeps its column name. An email shows a line with who sent it to whom, when and the subject, then its paragraphs; the quoted earlier messages follow under "Earlier in the thread", and signatures and attachments are left out (upload an attachment as its own source). These anchors are how each step on the map will point back to its evidence. PDF pages with almost no text (probably scanned) are listed; Claude reads them when you create the draft map. Pictures found in PDFs are saved without any hidden camera or location details and suggested on the matching steps when you create the draft map (see **Pictures on steps** below).
5. A passage that tries to give instructions to an AI (for example "ignore all previous instructions") carries the tag **Instruction-like text. Treated as data and ignored.** The app treats it as ordinary text in the document.
6. The box at the bottom says **Ready to draft** (with a link, **Go to Draft**) or lists exactly what is still needed, for example "Map 2 speakers in interview_transcript.txt".
7. **Delete** on a row removes that file and its passages after a confirmation.
8. **Emails:** the app hides every email address, and every person named in the From, To and Cc lines, as soon as the email is read. Those names are tagged "from an email" in **Names to hide**; team mailboxes such as "LC Ops Desk" stay visible. Open the email and use the **Sender mapping** card (like the speaker mapping, without Interviewer) to give each sender their role. Their paragraphs then show the role, and the role is what Claude sees.

### Interviews: speakers and names, step by step
The app splits an interview into who said what and when. It reads `[06:40] Name: text` or `Name: text` lines in text, Word and PDF files, Otter.ai text exports (the speaker and time on one line, such as "Speaker 1  0:06", with the words below), and the speakers in .vtt and .srt subtitle files. Otter often labels people "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2" …: map each to a role as usual, and add the names people use in the conversation (including misspellings the transcription made) to **Names to hide**.
1. Add the roles on **Setup** first (for example "Load controller").
2. On **Sources**, open the interview. The **Speaker mapping** card lists each speaker as written in the file, with how many passages they have. For each, choose their **Role**, or **Interviewer** for the person asking the questions. Click **Save speakers**. The passages in the viewer now show the role next to each answer.
3. **Names to hide** (below the speaker mapping) lists the names the app keeps out of view. Speaker names that look like a person's name are added automatically, marked "from speaker label", and shown as their role once you map them (as "[name]" before that). Add anyone else who is mentioned by name, and nicknames or first names used on their own (for example "Priya" as well as "Priya Nair"), under **Name to hide**, optionally with what to show instead. The × removes a name from the list.
4. Names appear in only three places: the speaker mapping, the names list and the correction editor. Everywhere else, including file names, the app shows the role or "[name]". Names never go into the map, exports or anything sent to Claude.
5. To fix words the transcript got wrong, click **Correct the text** in the viewer, then **Correct** on a passage, change the text and click **Save correction**. Corrected passages are tagged **Corrected** and are kept if the file is read again. Click **Done correcting** to go back.

### Draft, step by step
1. Open **Draft** in the top bar (or **Go to Draft** on Sources). **Before drafting** lists what the draft needs: your Claude API key saved, at least one interview transcript read, and every speaker mapped to a role. Each shows **Done** or **To do** with a link to fix it, and "Still needed" says exactly what is missing. A process document (an SOP or manual) is **Recommended**, not required: you can draft from interviews alone.
   - **Signal strength** (three small bars plus a word) says how well the sources can support the map: **Strong** (an interview and an SOP or manual), **Moderate** (an interview with only job descriptions, work records or other documents) or **Limited** (interviews only). It never stops you drafting or publishing. A Limited map means every step rests on what people said: confirm the steps with a document or another SME before relying on it; the quality card shows this as a warning. The signal also appears on the finished draft, next to the version on Review, Published and Exports, and in the Master Process Map and HTML exports.
2. Click **Create draft map**. If something is still missing, the app says what instead of starting.
3. While Claude works you see all seven stages with their status and how long each has taken:
   - **Reading the documents** (including scanned PDF pages);
   - **Extracting steps from each source**;
   - **Checking every quote against your documents**;
   - **Comparing sources**: the claims are grouped into steps and each source is compared with them (agrees, adds detail, differs, contradicts, or says nothing);
   - **Independent verification**: each step is checked a second time against only its own evidence;
   - **Building the map**: lanes, phases, the order of steps, who is responsible and accountable, times, constraints and where AI might help;
   - **Writing validation questions** for your SME.

   If you switched off the independent verifier in Settings, that stage shows a dash and **Switched off in Settings**. Below the stages: **Claims verified**, **Rejected** and **Tokens used so far**, updating every second. You can leave the screen; drafting carries on, and the screen picks it up again when you come back.
4. When it is done, **Draft v0.1** says how many checked claims it was built from and shows **Steps drafted**, **Needs check** (steps the verifier only partly supported), **Rejected claims** and **Open questions**. **Start reviewing** takes you to Review. If no steps could be drafted, it says so instead and suggests what to add before drafting again. The home screen card and the **Review** step in the top bar now show how many steps are waiting for your review.
5. **Rejected claims** lists every claim that failed a check, struck through, with the reason in red (for example "number not found in cited text: 30"), the check that caught it (for example **G5 · S1**), the file and passage, and **Open source** (or "Passage missing" when Claude cited a passage that doesn't exist). These never reach the map. The line above the list also says how many names in Claude's answers were replaced by roles and how many instruction-like passages were ignored.
6. If drafting stops, the stage that failed is marked **Failed**, the message says what happened and that nothing was saved, with **Try again** and **Copy details**.
7. **Draft again from sources** makes a fresh draft that replaces this one, with the next number (v0.2, v0.3 …), as long as you haven't reviewed any step. Once you have, use **Refresh draft from sources** on Review instead (see below): it keeps every step you reviewed.
8. Checks behind the scenes: a step is only kept if its claims passed every check; people's names never reach the map; a source that doesn't mention a step counts as silent, never as a contradiction; job descriptions never change how well a step is supported; every decision and approval is marked rule-based or judgment, and a judgment step is never suggested for automation. Each step's confidence is worked out from its evidence, not guessed by Claude.

### Review, step by step
1. Open **Review** in the top bar (or **Start reviewing** on Draft). Without a draft it says so and links to Draft.
2. **The banner** at the top shows the draft's version (for example **Draft v0.1**), how many sources it was made from, and **n of N steps reviewed** with one small segment per step in process order: grey until reviewed, green once reviewed, darker grey when rejected. Click a segment to open that step.
3. **Four cards** show the **Trigger**, the **End point**, the **Cycle time** along the usual route, the way most cases go, never through an exception route or a workaround (work plus waiting, with a red line such as "Incomplete: 1 step without time" when some steps have no time) and the **Process KPI** ("Not stated" if Setup has none).
4. **The map** is drawn from the backend's layout: one lane per role (lanes of approvals and exception steps sit just above the lane they come from), phases along the top, and steps in process order from left to right.
   - **Shapes:** rounded box = activity, diamond = decision (its name underneath), pill = approval, filled dot = start, double ring = end.
   - **Marks** (never more than three per step): a dashed outline = not reviewed yet; an orange dot = a key constraint; a small picture = the step has pictures.
   - **Lines:** arrows show the order. Dashed lines are loops back (rework or re-plan). Every exit of a decision or approval is labelled (Yes, No, Approve, Rework …). A dashed line labelled **Not described** means the interview jumps from one step to a later one without saying what happens in between; the app never invents a step there, and asks you a question instead.
   - **View: As done · As documented.** The map opens **As done**: the work as the people who do it describe it. **As documented** shows the version in your SOPs and job descriptions. Steps only one side mentions appear only in that view (on the sample, the Load control lead's plan approval is in the documents only, and reading the report at the aircraft on a busy wave is in practice only). Tick **Show the other view** to see the other view's steps as faint dashed outlines labelled "only in the documented version" or "only in practice". Choosing a step that exists only in the other view (from the progress bar, with J and K, or when the next step opens) switches to that view for you. Where the two views differ on a step, the line under it shows the current view's value (for example the time "Up to fifteen minutes' wait for the report" As done, and "Within 10 minutes of loading completion" As documented). The cycle time in the cards is the current view's.
   - **Lenses** change only the line under each step: **Plain** (its time, or "No time stated" in red), **Source state** (how well the sources agree, as a symbol with its name), **Confidence** (a bar and the value, orange and red below 0.60), **Time** (work as a solid bar, waiting as a hatched bar, and the minutes), **Work type** (the kind of work as a shape and a word: Clear, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic, Confused or Not classified, marked "proposed" until you confirm it) and **Exceptions** (one word per step: **Usual route**, **Exception** for a step only reached when a check fails, **Variation** for another way of working under a condition the interviewee gave, or **Workaround** for work done another way with the interviewee's reason; the line into the step says when). With **System records** switched on there is a seventh lens, **Volume and wait** (key **7**): "108 cases/mo · wait 39 min (median)" from the system records matched to the step, "too few cases" below 5, or "No system data". The app remembers your lens. The legend under the map explains the shapes, the lines and the current lens.
   - **Fit** shrinks the map to the width of the screen; **100%** shows it full size and lets you scroll sideways. The line above the map counts steps, lanes and phases, and turns red with "Large map: consider splitting by phase" above 25 steps or 6 lanes.
5. **Click a step** (or use Tab to move through the steps in process order and Enter to choose one). Its lines turn dark so you can follow its flow, and the panel on the right shows:
   - what kind of step it is, its phase and lane, and chips for how it was found (**Observed · O** or **Inferred · I**), how well the sources agree (**Contested** in red when they disagree), any **Divergence** (for example "threshold" or "ownership"), its **Confidence** and **Unreviewed**;
   - **Evidence:** one card per source. Cards that support or contradict the step show the quoted words and, in red, what differs (for example "Threshold divergence: 10 min in the SOP vs up to 15 min wait in practice"). A source that says nothing is shown in grey as **Silent**: silence is never counted as a contradiction;
   - **How confidence was computed:** each part of the score (for example "Observed (O) +0.60", "Capped at 0.60 while contested −0.10") and the total, which always equals the confidence shown;
   - **Role and accountability:** each role's letter (R responsible, A accountable, C consulted, I informed). When the sources disagree about who is accountable, it shows **A?** in red and names the sources on each side;
   - **Decision rights** (for decisions and approvals: rule-based or judgment, who decides, the rule and the escalation);
   - **Work type:** the SME's own word first, if a story gave one (for example "Firefighting", about one night), then the kind of work with the quoted words that show it; for complex work, what the SME watches and their rules of thumb;
     On a draft, **Confirm work type** keeps the proposed kind of work (the step itself still waits for your review), and **Change work type** lets you pick Clear, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic or Confused (a change counts as your edit, so the step is locked like any edit). Choosing Complex or Chaotic removes an automation flag and makes a decision a judgment call, because such work is never automated. Your choice is kept when you refresh the draft;
   - **Know-how:** artefacts, skills, rules of thumb, experience and natural talent (only ever for a role, never a person), each with where it was said, and the knowledge risk with why;
   - **Stories:** the stories the SME told about the step, in their own words, with how often it happens; a story told once is kept and marked **Single account**;
   - **Official vs lived:** what the SME said is meant to happen beside what actually happens, with the reason they gave and the interview question each answer came from; "Practice not described" when only the official account exists;
   - **Handoff**, **Time**, **KPI**, **Key challenges and constraints**, **Knowledge risk** and the **AI flag**, where they apply.
   Every place reference (like **29:48**) opens that passage.
6. **Click a piece of evidence** to open that source in the panel at the exact passage: the passage is framed and the quoted words highlighted. People's names are always replaced by roles. **← Back to [step]** (or Esc) returns to the step.
7. **Close details** shows **How to review** again, with **Start with the first step that needs attention**.

### Review, step by step (reviewing each step)
1. **Start with the first step that needs attention** opens the first step the verifier only partly supported, else the first unreviewed step.
2. Under the step's name you see what to do:
   - **Confirm as drafted** locks the step: refreshing the draft never changes it. A red line warns you first when the independent verifier found only partial support.
   - **Edit** opens the edit form (below).
   - **Reject step** fades it on the map; the map checks then show which steps lost their connection. **Restore** brings it back as unreviewed.
   After Confirm, Save or Reject, the next step that needs you opens by itself (steps needing a check first, then in process order). When none is left, **All steps reviewed** says so.
3. **The edit form** has every field of the step: name, kind, lane, who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed, work and waiting minutes, time window, complexity, KPI, decision rights (rule-based or judgment, who decides, the rule, the escalation), handoffs, constraints (add or remove), knowledge risk and the AI flag. The name and an Accountable role are required, and an activity needs a Responsible role. A field that isn't right gets a red border and a message just below it, and nothing is saved until everything is right. A judgment step can never be marked as an automation candidate, and a name from your hidden-names list is refused. **Cancel** or Esc throws the changes away. Only what you changed is saved, and the step becomes **Edited** (locked).
4. **Add a missing step after this one** (at the bottom of the details), or **More → Add missing step** above the map: give it a name, kind, lane and where it goes, and say where it comes from. **Observed in a source** needs a passage: **Pick a passage**, choose the source, and click **Use this passage** next to the right one (you can pick several). **Recommended practice** needs the reference you are relying on. The new step is **Added**, locked, and placed straight into the flow.
5. **Map | Table:** the table lists every step with its kind, lane, provenance, source state, time, confidence and review status. Filter it by **Needs check**, **Unreviewed** or **Contested**. Click a row to open the step. **Edit in place** changes the name, Responsible, Accountable, minutes and KPI without leaving the table; everything else is in the full edit form.
6. **Tabs under the map:**
   - **RACI matrix:** every step against every role; **A?** marks a disputed accountable role. Click a row to open that step.
   - **Validation questions:** the questions for your SME session, grouped by phase, with why each was asked. **Go to [step]**, **Mark answered** or **Reopen**. **Copy all** puts them on the clipboard and **Download .md** saves them as a file for the session. **Download validation pack** saves the whole validation session plan as a file: walk the As done steps with the SME ("Is this how it actually happens?"), a dissent round ("Imagine this map is wrong…") then an assent round, up to three steps where what is done and what is documented differ, shown side by side without saying which is right, the strengths and weaknesses to check, and the open questions. Enter what you hear as edits here afterwards.
   - **Rejected claims:** every claim that failed a check, with the reason. **Open passage** shows the passage; if the step is real, **Add as step from this passage** starts a new step with that passage already picked.
   - **Audit log:** every change, newest first: time, what was done, which step and by whom.
   - **Merge decisions:** where Claude thought two descriptions might be the same step, side by side with their quotes. **Accept merge**, **Keep separate**, or **Split** a merge again.
   - **Map checks:** what must be fixed before publishing (for example a decision with an unlabelled exit) and what is worth a look, each with **Go to step**.
7. **Keyboard:** **J** and **K** move to the next and previous step, **C** confirms, **E** edits, **R** rejects, **1** to **6** switch lens (**7** for Volume and wait when System records is on), **Esc** closes the source or a form, and **?** shows the list. They do nothing while you are typing in a field.
8. **More → Refresh draft from sources** (for example after adding a source): Claude reads every source again and redrafts only the steps you haven't reviewed. Steps you confirmed, edited, added or rejected stay exactly as they are, questions you marked answered stay answered, and the draft gets the next number (v0.2). The Draft screen shows its progress.
   - **Before you refresh**, the dialog compares today's setup with the one that made this draft. It says so if the model, the effort, the independent verifier or Claude's instructions (after an app update) have changed since, because fresh suggestions may then differ from the ones already on the map. A verifier switched off is named on its own: fresh suggestions would miss that second check. It never stops you; to refresh the way the draft was made, change Settings first. When nothing changed it says "Claude's model, settings and instructions are the same as when this draft was made."
   - **After the refresh**, the Draft screen's summary has a **What the refresh changed** section: how many reviewed steps stayed as they were, and among the steps you hadn't reviewed which are **New**, **No longer drafted** or **Renamed** (old name → new name), or that they came out the same. Look at those on the map before you confirm them.

1. Open a step on **Review**. Its details end with **Images** ("In-app only · never exported · metadata stripped").
2. **Add image to this step** opens a short form: choose the picture (JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 10 MB), write a caption using role names rather than people's names, choose what kind of picture it is (workplace photo, system screenshot, completed form or record, illustrative diagram or extract from a document) and tick **Faces, names, badge numbers and flight identifiers are removed or not shown**. A suggestion has a dashed border and says which file and page it came from. **Keep** asks for its type and the privacy tick (the caption is filled in for you, and you can change it); **Discard** hides it for good. Publishing waits until every suggestion is kept or discarded (the quality card's "Fully reviewed" check counts them). A step you reject stops counting.
4. **Remove** takes away a picture you added, after asking you first.
5. **On the map**, a step with pictures has a small picture mark at its top-left corner. Under the map, the **Visual walkthrough** shows every kept picture in process order ("Step 3 · Draft load plan" and the caption); click a card to open that step.
6. On **Published**, pictures and the walkthrough are shown but can't be changed. **Reopen as new draft** keeps them.

### Coverage, Matrix and Registers, step by step
1. **Coverage (Sources):** after a draft map is made, the **Coverage** column says how much of each file the map uses (for example "50%": half of its passages are cited by checked claims). Interviewer questions are not counted. Open a file to see a bar with "50% cited by the map"; passages nothing cites carry a dashed **Uncited** tag, and **Show uncited passages** lists only those, so you can spot anything the draft skipped. A file added after the draft shows "–" until the next draft or refresh.
2. **Matrix:** one row per step, one column per kind of source (SOP / manual, **SME official** (what interviewees say should happen), **SME lived** (what they say they actually do), Job description, Executed artefacts), then the step's state and any disagreement. Where an interview mentions a step only in answers that are neither clearly official nor clearly lived, both SME columns say **Account unclear**: the app never guesses which. Each cell is a symbol with its word: **Supports**, **Contradicts**, **Silent** (the source doesn't mention the step, which is never counted as a contradiction) or **No source of this kind**. Use the **Divergence** buttons (Sequence, Ownership, Threshold, Trigger, Exception, Omission) to show only steps with that kind of disagreement, and **Most serious first** to put contradictions and disagreements at the top. Click a step to open it on Review.
3. **Registers:** choose a register at the top. **K-RISK** (steps only a few people know how to do), **AI flags** (in the fixed five fields; a judgment step's flag is support only), **Constraints by cause** (grouped by cause type, key ones marked), **External practice (R)** (steps you added from a reference), **Validation questions** (copy or download them for your SME session), **Rejected claims** (**Open passage** opens the file on Sources), **Stories** (what happened in real cases, in the interviewees' own words, marked "Told once" when only one person told it), **Glossary** (the interviewees' own terms with their plain meaning), **Preserve / Improve** (what works well and what gets in the way, grouped by skillset, ways of working, systems, process and structure), the **Cause library**, **Emerging themes** and **Lane boundaries**. Every entry has **Go to [step]**. **Emerging themes** are patterns across two or more stories that fit no existing category: each shows what the stories have in common (marked as inferred) and the stories themselves; **Accept**, **Rename** (accepts it under your name for it), **Dismiss** or **Reopen**. A theme needs at least two stories, so a single story never becomes one. Themes never change the map, and a new draft or refresh never touches the ones you decided. **Exceptions and workarounds** shows, for the As done view (switch to As documented to compare), how much of the process is the usual route and how much is exception handling: a line of facts (for example "3 of 9 steps (33%) are off the usual route … 2 rework loops; 1 workaround. The exception routes add 4 hand-overs"), each exception route and variation (what starts it, the steps and lanes, where it rejoins or loops back, the extra hand-overs, how often it happens in the interviewee's words, and the reason they gave), the rework loops, the workarounds with the interviewee's reason, and **Still to ask**: "How often does something like that happen?" for each route nobody has put a frequency on. These questions also go into Follow-up guides. Nothing here changes the map. **Lane boundaries** lists the steps where the interviews say more than one team does the work (**Shared delivery**) or that it falls between teams (**Falls between teams**): each shows the one **Responsible** the map keeps (the step's lane), the other teams as **Consulted**, the guide questions that raised it, and a note to settle it with the teams. These also become questions in Follow-up guides. Nothing here changes the map.
4. **Cause library:** every step where the sources disagree gets an entry when a draft is made or refreshed. It says what differs (a fact from your sources) and, only when the step's own constraints name one, a possible cause, clearly marked as an inference. Otherwise it says "Cause not known yet: ask your SME". **Confirm** or **Refute** each entry after the SME session (**Reopen** undoes it). Entries are never duplicated by a refresh, and your verdicts are kept.
5. Matrix and Registers show the draft you are working on, or the latest published version when there is no draft. Nothing on these screens changes the map.

### Gap analysis, step by step (optional)
1. Open **Gap analysis** in the top bar (marked **Optional**). It works on the draft you are working on, or the latest published map; **Map** lets you choose another version. Nothing here changes the map by itself.
2. **Documents vs practice:** click **Run analysis** (about 2 AI calls). Claude looks only at the steps where your sources disagree and the passages behind them. Every finding must quote passages from both sides (the documents and what the SMEs said; a source that says nothing counts as silent), every quote, number and role is checked against your files, and the independent verifier checks each finding again. Anything that fails is listed under **Rejected findings** with the reason, never shown as a finding.
3. **The report** has three parts: **Facts** (where the documents and practice differ, with a severity: ▲ High, ◆ Medium, ○ Low), **Interpretation** (patterns or likely causes, only when what the SMEs said supports them) and **Recommendation** (updates the documents need). When the sources don't explain a cause, it appears under **Questions for your SMEs** instead of a guess.
   - **Reality gaps** are findings where the official and the lived version of the work differ: a **Known adaptation** (the SME says how it is meant to work, then what they actually do), a **Rule doesn't fit the work** (a fixed rule for a call the SME says has no fixed answer), and others. Each carries a **gap class**: effective adaptation (worth writing down), risky drift (worth correcting), obsolete rule (worth retiring) or unexplained. Claude may suggest a class only when the SME gave a reason, quoted word for word; otherwise it stays "unexplained" and a question asks the SMEs why. You choose the class you keep in **Gap class to keep** when you decide on the finding. Filter by type, severity, evidence strength or decision. Click a piece of evidence to open its source, or **Open [step] on the map**.
4. **Decide on every finding:** **Add as validation question** (it joins the questions on the draft), **Add to cause library** (documents vs practice only), **Add as step** (choose its name, kind, lane and where it goes; it is added to the draft as your own step, from the finding's passages or, for external findings, with the source as its reference) or **Dismiss** (you must say why). Questions and steps go into the current draft; if the map is published and there is no draft, the app asks you to **Reopen as new draft** first. Every decision is recorded in the audit log.
5. When every finding has a decision, **Finalise report** makes it read-only, and **Export .md** downloads it (facts, interpretation, recommendation; role names only, no passage quotes, no names). Finalised reports are listed in the Master Process Map's section 3 when that version is published, and on **Exports**.
6. **External best practice** (needs **Web research** switched on in Settings): click **Prepare research brief**. The app drafts a generic brief from your map: the process, your industry and adjacent industries (from Setup), the steps without any names, times or thresholds, and four research questions. Edit anything, choose **Most searches** (3 to 20; each is billed by Anthropic), limit the sources to certain sites or leave some out, and set how old sources may be.
7. **Confidentiality check:** click **Save and check**. Anything that must not go to the web is highlighted: a name to hide, a confidential term from Setup, a number or time from your map, or 8 or more words copied from your sources. Click **Remove** on each. Then tick **This brief contains no confidential information** and click **Run research**. Only the brief is sent; your transcripts, documents and map never are. If anything still blocks the run, the screen says exactly what.
8. **External findings** come only from pages the web search returned in the same run: each shows the source's title, site, type (standards body, regulator, industry association …), date and a short excerpt, with **Open source**. Findings backed only by vendor or unknown sites are marked **weak evidence**, old sources **dated**, and anything about legal or regulatory requirements carries "Confirm with your compliance team; this is not legal advice." Practices your map already follows are left out as "not a gap". If the search limit was reached, a note says the report may be incomplete. Decide on each finding as in step 4, then finalise and export.
9. A step's details on Review show a **Gap findings** section when any finding is about it, and the Review toolbar shows **n open gap findings** while decisions are waiting.

### System records, step by step (optional)
1. System records is off until you switch it on: **Settings → Optional modules → System records**. Then **System records** (marked **Optional**) appears in the top bar after **Sources**. Switching it off hides it again and deletes nothing.
2. **Ask IT for an extract:** download the data request template (**Word**, **Markdown** or the **CSV header**) and send it to the system owner. It asks for roles rather than user IDs, no comments and no customer details, and has a box for the data owner's approval.
3. **Upload a log:** choose the CSV or Excel (.xlsx) file (for Excel, the first sheet is read, with the column names in its first filled row), give it a name (and the system, the time zone and the date window if you know them) and click **Upload**. The file stays in memory only until you import it; if the app is closed before then, upload it again.
4. **Import, in five steps:** (1) **Map columns**: check which column is the case ID, activity and timestamp (the app suggests them; columns left out are never read), and change the date format only if dates can't be read; (2) **Time zone** of the times in the file; (3) **Map users to roles**: choose a role, or Unknown role, for each user ID. The IDs show only on this step ("These are not stored"): the import keeps the role and forgets the ID; (4) **Validation report**: rows read and kept, cases, activities, dates and why any rows were dropped. If the import would be refused, it says why and how to fix the extract; (5) tick **The data owner approved this extract for this purpose**, type the approver's role (a role, not a name) and click **Import**.
5. **Logs** lists each log with its status, date window, cases and events. **Open** shows an imported log in three tabs; **Delete** removes a log, its events and its figures.
6. **Matching to map steps** (needs a draft map): each activity the system records, with the step the app proposes, why ("Same words: plan. Same role: Load controller.") and its status. **Confirm** accepts the proposal, **Choose another step** lets you pick the step yourself, **Hidden work** says it is work the map doesn't show. Below, the steps no activity is matched to: **Confirm no system record** says the step is done offline or not recorded. Nothing changes the map until you confirm; confirmed matches show on the map as the order the system records. **Ask Claude for suggestions** (about 1 AI call) proposes steps for the activities the app couldn't match; Claude sees only activity names, volumes and roles, and its suggestions still wait for you.
7. **Metrics**: all cases (start to finish, rework, handoffs, variants), volume and waits per activity, the waits and handoffs between activities, the top 10 variants with how many cases they cover, and figures per role. Times are the median and the 90th percentile. Anything resting on fewer than 5 cases is never shown; each table says how many were left out. **Import report** shows the final validation report.
8. **On Review** (with System records on): a step's details gain **System data**: its volume, cases, the median and 90th-percentile wait and work, rework, the variants through it, the log and its dates, and the stated times beside the measured one ("SME estimate … · SOP … · measured median 13.4 min"). The **Variants** tab under the map lists the most common routes through the cases, each saying whether it fits **As done** and **As documented** and, if not, why; click a step to open it. **Hidden work** lists the work the system records that no step shows; **Add as step** opens Add missing step filled in (name, lane, after which step), with the system record as its evidence (you can add a passage too).
9. **Linked logs** (once two logs are imported): when two systems give the same case different IDs, for example a flight number in the departure control system and a turnaround reference in the ramp system, ask IT for a file of pairs: the first column the first log's case IDs, the second column the second log's, with a header row (CSV or Excel). Choose the **First log** and **Second log**, **Choose file**, **Link logs**. The card then says how many pairs each log knows and the **end-to-end time across both systems** (median and 90th percentile, measured), never for fewer than 5 linked cases. A new file for the same two logs replaces the link; **Unlink** removes it; deleting either log removes it too. Without a link, each log is analysed on its own.

## How to use it
A typical engagement, start to finish (each step has its own section above):
1. **Settings** (once): save your name and your Claude API key, and click **Test connection**.
2. **New engagement** on the home screen, then **Setup**: the trigger, the end point and the roles, in lane order.
3. **Interview**: download a standard template, hold the interview with it and say each question ID as you ask it.
4. **Sources**: upload the interview transcripts, SOPs, manuals and job descriptions; map each speaker to a role and check the **Names to hide** list.
5. **Draft**: click **Create draft map** and wait for the seven stages (a few minutes, depending on how much you uploaded).
6. **Review**: confirm, edit, reject or add every step; take the **Questions** tab to your SME and come back with the answers; **Refresh draft from sources** after you add or correct a source.
7. **Publish** when the quality card is green, then download what you need on **Exports**.
8. When useful: **Matrix** and **Registers** to see where sources agree and where knowledge sits, **Gap analysis** for a second opinion, and **Leadership** to brief senior readers on a published map.
9. After the SME session, **Reopen as new draft** on **Published**, make the changes and publish the next version.

## Glossary
- **Systemic cause:** a cause (such as system fragmentation) that holds up steps in two or more lanes or phases, so it needs a decision above any one team.
- **Local cause:** a cause that holds up steps in one lane and one phase only.
- **Evidence weight:** how many of the map's sources support the steps a cause holds up, for example "Supported by 3 of 5 sources".
- **Recoverable wait:** the waiting time on steps held up by a systemic cause; the time that fixing those causes could win back.
- **Engagement:** one process you are mapping, with its documents and map versions.
- **SME:** subject-matter expert; the person interviewed about how the work is done.
- **Swimlane map:** a process map with one horizontal lane per role.
- **Provenance (O / I / R):** where a step came from: observed in a source, inferred from a source, or recommended from external practice (added only by you).
- **API key:** the password-like code that lets the app use Claude on your Anthropic account. It stays on your computer.
- **Fixture:** illustrative sample data used to test the app. It is not real SATS data.
- **Model:** the version of Claude that reads your documents (for example Claude Opus 5.5).
- **Effort:** how much thinking Claude does on each task; higher is more thorough, slower and costs more.
- **Independent verifier:** a second check of every drafted step against only its own evidence.
- **Copy details:** a button on error messages that copies a short code for a bug report.
- **Anchor:** the short reference to a passage in a source, such as §3.1, p. 5 or ¶ 7.
- **Lane:** one horizontal band of the map, for one role.
- **Confidential terms:** names that must never be sent to web research.
- **Gap analysis:** an optional, advisory comparison of your map with your own documents (documents vs practice) or with published practice (external best practice). It never changes the map by itself.
- **Research brief:** the short, generic description of your process that is the only thing sent to web search.
- **Confidentiality check:** the scan of the research brief for names, confidential terms, map numbers and copied passages; the research can't run until it is clean and you have ticked that the brief holds nothing confidential.
- **Source tier:** the kind of website a source comes from (standards body, regulator, industry association, academic, peer operator, consultancy, vendor, news or other).
- **Finding decision:** what you choose to do with a gap finding: add it as a validation question, add it to the cause library, add it as a step, or dismiss it with a reason.
- **Speaker mapping:** telling the app which role each speaker in an interview has (or that they are the interviewer).
- **Names to hide (redaction list):** people's names the app replaces with their role or "[name]" everywhere except three owner-only places.
- **Instruction-like text:** a passage that reads like an instruction to an AI; it is kept as ordinary text and never followed.
- **Source state:** how well a step is supported: corroborated (interview and documents agree), practiced only (interview only), documented only (documents only), contested (sources disagree) or trace contradicted (a record of the work disagrees).
- **Needs check:** a step the independent verifier found only partly supported by its evidence.
- **Lens:** a way of reading the map that changes only the line under each step (time, source agreement, confidence).
- **Divergence:** a difference between sources about a step, such as a time threshold (threshold) or who owns it (ownership).
- **RACI:** Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed: who does, owns, advises on and hears about each step.
- **Validation question:** a question for your SME about a step whose sources disagree, that only one kind of source describes, or that nobody could answer from the documents.
